\begin{abstract}
Edge intrusion-detection studies can report optimistic results when
preprocessing, checkpoint selection, repeated runs, and deployment evidence
are not bound to the same protocol. We present a fail-closed evaluation of
ReLU MLP, quantized-activation (QCFS) MLP, and TinyCNN classifiers on
NSL-KDD, UNSW-NB15, CICIDS2017, and IoT-23. Every arm uses the same immutable
fit/validation/test cache and \vFiveSeeds\ paired seeds. Encoders, scaling,
class weights, and quantization calibration are fit without test data; each
checkpoint is the fixed final epoch, with validation used only for diagnostics.
A second independent execution must
match the complete training-state digest before any test evaluation is
allowed, after which logits, probabilities, predictions, and recomputed
metrics must also match. We separate tests for a difference from tests for a
run-frozen \vFiveMargin\ percentage-point equivalence margin and control
each family with Holm's method. Random Forest and XGBoost references use the
same caches and test barrier. ONNX/QDQ validation is checkpoint-bound software
evidence, not proof of NPU mapping or physical-board latency. This design
makes the numerical claims reproducible while limiting CICIDS2017 and IoT-23
conclusions to fixed, exact-input-group-disjoint pattern benchmarks rather than
unseen-device, capture, or future-time generalization.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Network intrusion detection at an IoT edge node must balance minority-class
performance, predictable resource use, and deployability. Those objectives
are difficult to compare when published values originate from different data
splits or deployment measurements are not traceable to the evaluated
checkpoint. The problem is especially acute for datasets containing repeated
flows or capture-specific artifacts: a conventional random row split is a
useful benchmark but is not evidence of generalization to a new device or time
period.

This work emphasizes evidence boundaries rather than a claimed universal
winner. Its contributions are:

\begin{itemize}
  \item a source-hash and schema audit for four multi-class datasets, followed
  by fit-only categorical encoding, scaling, and class weighting;
  \item a fixed-final-epoch, \vFiveSeeds-seed protocol with a global
  primary/replica barrier that prevents test evaluation until every training
  job is independently reproduced;
  \item paired difference and equivalence analyses that do not equate a
  non-significant difference with equivalence;
  \item leakage-safe Random Forest and XGBoost references on NSL-KDD and
  UNSW-NB15; and
  \item checkpoint-bound FP32 ONNX and QDQ gates whose scope excludes vendor
  compilation, physical-board latency, and energy unless those artifacts are
  separately supplied.
\end{itemize}

Earlier exploratory results from this repository exposed test data during
checkpoint selection. They are not reused here. Historical MCU timing files
also are not attributed to the version-5 checkpoints because they lack the
required checkpoint-to-binary provenance chain.

\section{Related Work}

NSL-KDD remains common in resource-constrained IDS research despite its origin
in older DARPA traffic~\cite{tavallaee2009nslkdd}. UNSW-NB15 adds a more
modern ten-class setting~\cite{moustafa2015unsw}, CICIDS2017 supplies a larger
flow corpus~\cite{sharafaldin2018cicids}, and IoT-23 contains IoT botnet
captures~\cite{garcia2020iot23}. CICIDS2017 has documented flow-construction
and labeling problems~\cite{engelen2021cicids}; preprocessing hygiene cannot
repair those upstream limitations.

Prior edge deployments span specialized AI MCUs, conventional MCUs,
neuromorphic ASICs, SBCs, and FPGAs. Ngo et al. deployed a small network on
MAX78000~\cite{ngo2023hhnids}; Chehade et al. evaluated a CNN on
STM32F7~\cite{chehade2025stm32}; and Diab et al. used Raspberry Pi
hardware~\cite{diab2025rpi}. Their tasks, label spaces, preprocessing, and
hardware differ, so their latency and accuracy values are context rather than
head-to-head baselines. Here ``export-ready'' means that a selected
checkpoint can pass a reproducible export gate. It does not mean that an MCU
binary has been built or measured.

QCFS was proposed in ANN-to-SNN conversion work as a discretized activation
with multiple levels~\cite{bu2022optimal,jiang2023unified}. In this paper it
is evaluated as a quantized ANN activation. A finite-level QCFS output is not
silently identified with a one-step binary-spike neuron; an explicit temporal
simulation would be a separate experiment.

\section{Audited Data Protocol}

\subsection{Sources, roles, and labels}

The audit records the byte length and SHA-256 digest of every model input.
NSL-KDD retains the official train/test roles and maps attacks into five
declared classes. UNSW-NB15 uses the file roles indicated by content and the
official schema; its identifier is excluded and its categorical fields remain
available for fit-only encoding. CICIDS2017 uses all eight MachineLearningCSV
files. The three Web Attack label spellings containing the release archive's
replacement character are normalized by exact full-label aliases rather than
fuzzy matching. IoT-23 uses a pinned preprocessed artifact and preserves its
five-group historical mapping; this mapping is not described as a pure

\subsection{Split and preprocessing isolation}

The evaluation unit is a unique labelled final-FP32 input pattern, not a
traffic-frequency-weighted row. Canonical raw identities are recorded before
preprocessing. Exact-input groups determine assignment components, with
components merged when preprocessing creates final-FP32 collisions; no
component may cross fit, validation, and test. Conflicting labels remain
distinct labelled patterns within the same component.

NSL-KDD and UNSW-NB15 retain the official file roles. Validation is split only
from official training components; official-test components touching official
training data are excluded, and repeated labelled patterns are deduplicated.
CICIDS2017 and IoT-23 use approximate target proportions of 64/16/20 for
fit/validation/test. Grouped stratification fixes five folds and fold zero,
with validation seed 20260920 and combined-data test seed 42; group constraints
need not yield exact proportions. Every final split must contain each declared
class; a failed support check stops preparation instead of selecting another
fold.

Categorical vocabularies and the incremental standard scaler are fitted on
canonical-raw unique labelled fit patterns. They are not refitted after final
FP32 deduplication, so distinct raw patterns that collapse later can influence
the scaler more than once. Training, evaluation, and neural class-weight
counts instead use the final unique labelled patterns. Unknown validation/test
categories receive a declared sentinel. Declared missing or infinite numeric
values for the applicable datasets become zero before fit-only scaling;
unexpected numeric tokens fail the run. CICIDS2017 removes the verified
physical duplicate of \texttt{Fwd Header Length} and excludes destination port,
while retaining the remaining schema-defined constant features.

The available IoT-23 artifact does not retain reliable device, capture, or
time-group identifiers and was globally deduplicated upstream. Exact model-input
disjointness therefore does not establish device, capture, or time isolation,
or repair unknown upstream preprocessing. CICIDS2017 likewise is not a capture
holdout. We limit both claims accordingly.

\section{Models and Reproducible Training}

\subsection{Neural models}

The ReLU MLP contains three hidden affine blocks of widths 256, 256, and 128,
each followed by BatchNorm and ReLU, and a final class layer. The QCFS arm
replaces each ReLU with
\begin{equation}
 q(x)=\left(\frac{\left\lfloor L\,\mathrm{clip}(x/\theta,0,1)+\tfrac{1}{2}\right\rfloor}{L}\right)\theta,
 \qquad L=4,
\end{equation}
where each activation layer has a trainable threshold initialized to one,
and the straight-through estimator is used during training. Division by $L$
precedes multiplication by $\theta$ in the FP32 implementation. This corrected
shifted formula defines a new method version; checkpoints from the earlier
unshifted formula are incompatible. TinyCNN uses two one-dimensional
feature-axis convolutions implemented as $1\times3$ Conv2D layers with 8 and
16 channels, followed by a linear classifier. IoT-23 omits TinyCNN from the
declared primary arms.

All neural models use Adam, cosine decay, square-root inverse-frequency
fit-only class weighting, and FP32 deterministic operations. Weights are
computed from final-deduplicated fit labels and normalized to sum to the number
of classes. NSL-KDD, UNSW-NB15, and CICIDS2017 train
for 80 epochs with batch size 512; IoT-23 trains for 40 epochs with batch size
1024. Every ten epochs and at the final epoch, validation macro recall is
evaluated only as a diagnostic. The checkpoint is always the fixed final
epoch; neither validation nor test chooses an epoch or changes the training
budget.

\subsection{Independent-execution barrier}

The experiment plan freezes source hashes, environment, cache fingerprints,
hyperparameters, seed order, and bounded job concurrency. All 11 primary jobs
must finish before all 11 jobs are executed again in an independent namespace.
For every seed, the checkpoint tensors, optimizer, scheduler, RNG states,
batch order, and history contribute to a training digest. Any mismatch blocks
all test access. After the barrier, primary and replica checkpoints are each
evaluated on test. Their saved labels, logits, probabilities, predictions,
and independently recomputed metrics must produce the same scientific digest.
Only primary values appear in the tables.

Concurrency occurs across independent dataset/model jobs, never by changing
the model, batch size, precision, or seed order. Thus it improves utilization
without changing the scientific protocol.

\subsection{Tree references}

Random Forest uses 100 trees, maximum depth 20, balanced class weights, and
\vFiveNslRandomForestSeeds\ seeds. XGBoost uses 100 depth-6 histogram trees,
learning rate 0.1, and inverse-frequency fit-only sample weights. Because its
declared configuration has no row or column subsampling, one deterministic
XGBoost fit is reported rather than repeated identical values with artificial
zero uncertainty. Tree hyperparameters are fixed without validation or test
selection. A primary/replica semantic model digest barrier precedes test
prediction, and the Random Forest export gate requires an ONNX
\texttt{TreeEnsembleClassifier} with validation parity. The two Random Forest
exports, one per reference dataset, are separate from the neural export matrix
and do not require additional fits.

\section{Statistical Protocol}

The two primary metrics are overall accuracy and macro F1, both in percentage
points. All comparisons use paired seed identifiers and report ReLU minus the
comparator. The difference family contains ReLU--QCFS for four datasets and
ReLU--TinyCNN for three datasets, each for both metrics (14 hypotheses). It
uses a paired signed-rank randomization distribution with fixed tie handling,
followed by Holm adjustment across the full family. Its estimand is a symmetric
paired-difference location, summarized by the Hodges--Lehmann pseudomedian;
arithmetic mean differences are descriptive, not the signed-rank estimand.
The inference assumes independent paired seed differences symmetric about the
tested location. Exact conditional computation does not prove those assumptions.

Equivalence is a distinct eight-hypothesis family: four datasets by two
metrics for ReLU versus QCFS. Two one-sided paired $t$ tests use the frozen
$\pm\vFiveMargin$ percentage-point margin; the intersection--union value is
Holm-adjusted across all eight hypotheses. These tests target the mean paired
seed difference. The margin is a run-frozen numerical-equivalence sensitivity
margin, not an empirically established practical-importance threshold or
preregistration for earlier exploratory work. Difference non-rejection is not
equivalence, and a detected difference can coexist with numerical equivalence
under this margin.

A separate eight-hypothesis signed-rank TOST family assesses robustness for
the symmetric paired-difference location, with its own Holm adjustment. All
eight primary and robustness decisions, adjusted $p$ values, and their
discordances must be reported, including unfavorable or inconclusive outcomes.
The primary test is not switched after examining a normality test or a result.
Family-wise control is separate within the difference, primary-equivalence,
and robustness families, not a joint guarantee across all three families.

\section{Export and Deployment Evidence Boundary}

The neural export policy fixes deployment seed zero, BatchNorm folding, ONNX
opset 17, and export batch one before attempts. Export starts from the verified
checkpoint and the same cached feature order. Folding and FP32 ONNX execution
must match the original PyTorch logits with absolute tolerance $10^{-6}$,
relative tolerance $10^{-5}$, and zero prediction disagreement. ONNX is also
checked structurally. A fixed seeded, without-replacement sampling procedure
selects validation vectors and fit-only calibration rows; insufficient rows
stop the gate rather than silently reducing its sample sizes.

Static QDQ uses signed INT8 activations and weights, per-channel weights, and
MinMax calibration for Gemm, MatMul, and Conv operators. The recorded ONNX
Runtime CPU provider and recipe define the software gate, whose QDQ prediction
disagreement limit is fixed at one percent. Operator inventories, selected
vectors, outputs, disagreements, policy, and file hashes are sealed together.
All attempts, including numerical failures, are retained; resource or storage
errors are not treated as scientific quantization failures. A failed gate is
not repaired by selecting another seed or widening a threshold after inspection.

For the fixed seed-0 matrix, FP32 ONNX passed
\vExportFpPassed/\vExportFpTotal gates, while QDQ passed
\vExportQdqPassed/\vExportQdqTotal gates. Each gate uses
\vExportValidationVectors fixed validation vectors; QDQ calibration uses
\vExportCalibrationRows fit rows. The worst FP32 absolute logit error was
\vExportFpWorstAbsError. Among passing QDQ attempts, the largest
prediction-disagreement rate was
\vExportQdqWorstPassedDisagreementPct\%, against the frozen
\vExportQdqLimitPct\% limit. These sampled gates do not prove parity for
every possible input.

Only passing attempts support sampled software parity for their particular
checkpoint and the recorded ONNX Runtime stack, not portability of every
checkpoint or every possible input. QDQ nodes do not establish that every
operator executes in integer arithmetic or
on an NPU. A physical deployment claim additionally requires a pinned vendor
compiler report, mapped-operator report, firmware binary, clock evidence,
identical input vectors, board logits, raw timing logs, and, for energy, direct
voltage/current measurements. Those items are outside the automated training
suite and are not inferred from historical microbenchmarks in the repository.

\section{Limitations and Conclusion}

The deterministic replica test is evidence for this recorded software and
hardware stack, not a theorem of cross-platform bitwise reproducibility.
Twenty seeds characterize optimization variation under fixed splits; they do
not create independent datasets or additional rare-attack observations. The
CICIDS2017 fifteen-class aggregate is a fixed-pattern descriptive benchmark;
the protocol does not support claims of reliable Heartbleed, SQL-injection,
or Infiltration detection or population sampling precision.
Per-class supports must be disclosed from accepted metadata, not from dry
simulations. NSL-KDD is old, the original CICIDS2017
flows retain known upstream issues, and IoT-23's available preprocessing and
missing group identifiers prevent unseen-device/capture/time claims. The
study covers a shallow MLP, its QCFS variant, TinyCNN where declared, and two
tree references; it does not establish optimality over all IDS models.
L-sweeps, temporal IF simulations, and frozen-ANN layerwise diagnostics are
optional separate analyses, not completed ablations claimed by this study.

Within those boundaries, the work replaces leakage-prone and hand-maintained
numbers with an auditable chain from source hashes through fit-only
preprocessing, fixed-final-epoch checkpoints, independent retraining, test artifacts,
statistical families, and generated manuscript macros. The main scientific
result is therefore not an unconditional deployment claim: it is a
reproducible four-dataset comparison whose difference, equivalence, and export
claims can be accepted or rejected independently from their sealed evidence.
